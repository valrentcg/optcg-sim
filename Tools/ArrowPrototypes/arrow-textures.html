Arrow texture forge · channel-packed

Three assets built to the standard VFX layout: greyscale masks packed one per channel so a shader reads all of them in a single sample, plus a gradient LUT so colour is authored as a picture instead of a piecewise function. Tune, then download real PNGs. Import into Unity with sRGB (Color Texture) off for the masks and the LUT — they are data, not colour.

1 · Ribbon tiles vertically · x = across the width, y = along the length

R hard core · G soft halo · B grain & branches · A coverage for premultiplied. Branches follow the Substance trail recipe — brush noise split into individual strands rather than one uniform band.

2 · Arrowhead tip anchored at the right edge, mid-height

The tip is a vertex in an image. It cannot degrade at any zoom, because there is no field collapsing to zero — the halo channel simply keeps its own footprint past the point. The three silhouettes let the arrow's state read without colour: check the R channel, which is greyscale, and confirm you can still tell them apart.

3 · Gradient LUT 256 × 8 · greyscale in, colour out

Saturation is clamped away from both extremes — 100% blends into UI, 0% disappears into the board. The white point sits at hot point, not at 1.0, so the ramp keeps headroom instead of clipping.

4 · Erosion same UV space as the ribbon · drives the dissolve

Low values erode first. The bias makes the tail break up before the tip, so the arrow is consumed toward the target rather than dimming all at once — the uniform fade is the most recognisable tell of a programmer-made effect.

5 · Detail noise seamless on a torus · tiles in both axes · reusable

R fine fbm · G coarse fbm · B directional streaks. Sample two channels at different scales and scroll speeds and multiply — that parallax is most of the difference between a texture sliding along a beam and a beam that looks alive. Not arrow-specific: this is the detail source for the card burn and any other uGUI effect.
