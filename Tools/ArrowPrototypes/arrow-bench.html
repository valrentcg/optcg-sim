Why the bloom flattens

Every radius goes to zero at the junction — shaft half-width, both barb half-widths, and the pixel floors that taper with them. No layer has anything left to draw with at the point.

What survives is forwardCoverage, a smoothstep on dot(fromApex, backDir). That's a half-plane test, and a half-plane boundary is a straight line — so the bloom's leading edge is a straight line sitting exactly where the barbs meet the tip. The white core converges anyway because its radius multiplier is 1×, so the cut lands outside anything visible; the outer layer's 3.4× pushes its field past the cut, which is what you see.

Switch to radial to measure that falloff from the apex point instead of across the axis.

Rebuilt glow

Distance to the skeleton, normalised once by a constant radius. The halo becomes a uniform sheath around the whole arrow and caps the apex as a disc automatically — no tip special case, no pinch at the neck. The body underneath still tapers to a point.

End glow (old patch — off in rebuilt)

A floor under the half-width the BLUR layers use, so the outer and mid fields keep a radius where the ribbons converge. Runs through the same profiles as the body, so it matches. Fixes the shaft tip and both barb points at once. Core is untouched — the needle stays sharp.
